\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

Replica sets keep data available across failures, but a client may still meet
different versions as its requests move between members. We examined four
client-centric properties in MongoDB: read-your-writes, monotonic reads,
monotonic writes, and writes-follow-reads. Eight combinations of read concern,
write concern, and causal sessions were tested with property-specific schedules.
We then repeated selected configurations during secondary loss, primary
election, and a replication-path partition. The analysis covers 256
configuration histories and \RQTwoHistoryCount\ fault histories.

All normal controls passed. Under deliberately divergent replica states, weak
settings sometimes returned a completed stale or reordered result. In contrast,
the majority-acknowledged causal setting often left a dependent operation
unresolved. The partition exposed this difference most clearly: it produced
every completed fault-campaign violation, while simpler member failures did
not. Atomic write pre-images provided the evidence used to decide the two
write-ordering properties. The findings describe the tested schedules and
single-host deployment, not the frequency of these outcomes in general use.
