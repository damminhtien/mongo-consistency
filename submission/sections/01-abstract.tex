\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

Replication lets a database survive member failures, but it does not ensure
that a client sees successive operations in the order it expects. We chose
MongoDB because its read concern, write concern, and causal-session settings
let us change that client experience without changing the application
workload. In a three-member replica set, we tested read-your-writes, monotonic
reads, monotonic writes, and writes-follow-reads under normal operation,
controlled replica divergence, and three topology faults. We stated our
predictions before comparing the recorded histories with them.

The normal controls satisfied all four checks. When replicas diverged, weak
settings could return a completed stale or reordered result. The
majority-acknowledged causal setting often gave no decidable result instead:
an operation timed out or its dependency remained unresolved. Partitioning
the replication path made this contrast clearest; the two simpler member
failures produced no completed counterexample in the scheduled calls. These
observations fit the predicted distinction between a stale answer and a
withheld answer, but unresolved histories cannot confirm a consistency
guarantee. Our conclusions are limited to the tested schedules and the
single-host deployment.
