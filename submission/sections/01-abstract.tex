\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

We tested four client-centric consistency properties on a three-member MongoDB
replica set: read-your-writes (RYW), monotonic reads (MR), monotonic writes
(MW), and writes-follow-reads (WFR). The first set of schedules varied read
concern, write concern, and causal-session settings across eight configurations.
A separate fault campaign covered a secondary failure, a primary failure and
election, and a partition that isolated the former primary. In normal
operation, \NormalPassCount\ of
\NormalHistoryCount\ histories passed and \NormalViolationCount\ showed a
violation. Of \AdversarialHistoryCount\ adversarial histories,
\AdversarialPassCount\ passed and \AdversarialViolationCount\ violated a
property. A further \AdversarialUnavailableCount\ did not complete,
\AdversarialIndeterminateCount\ could not be resolved, and
\AdversarialPreconditionMissCount\ failed a precondition; precondition misses
were excluded from the consistency denominator. The fault campaign recorded
\RQTwoHistoryCount\ histories across \RQTwoFaultEpisodeCount\ fault episodes:
\RQTwoPassCount\ passed, \RQTwoViolationCount\ violated a property, and
\RQTwoIndeterminateCount\ remained indeterminate. All its violations were
recorded in the primary-isolating partition condition. In four test cells,
majority-write timeouts prevented the dependent operation from being
issued. The findings are limited to MongoDB \MongoDBServerVersion, PyMongo
\PyMongoVersion, the registered schedules, and the single-host topology.
