\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

Replication lets a database remain operational after member failures, but
clients may observe different states while updates propagate. We tested
read-your-writes (RYW), monotonic reads (MR), monotonic writes (MW), and
writes-follow-reads (WFR) across eight MongoDB consistency configurations on a
three-member replica set. The configuration campaign covered 256 histories
(96 normal controls and 160 adversarial histories); the fault campaign covered
432 histories across secondary loss, primary election, and replication-path
partition. We specified schedule-specific predictions for each
configuration-property pair and compared them with recorded histories.

All normal controls passed. Under adversarial schedules, C1 returned
counterexamples for all four properties. C6 produced no decidable histories
for its four properties: dependent reads timed out, while some write effects
remained unresolved. F1 calls and post-election F2 calls produced no
violations. F3 produced 56 violations in four configuration-property cells.
These findings apply to the tested schedules, timeout settings, and
single-host Docker deployment.
