\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

Replication lets a database remain operational after member failures, but
clients may observe different replica states while updates propagate. We
tested read-your-writes (RYW), monotonic reads (MR), monotonic writes (MW), and
writes-follow-reads (WFR) across eight MongoDB consistency configurations on a
three-member replica set. The configurations varied read concern, write
concern, and causal sessions. The configuration campaign covered 256 histories
(96 normal controls and 160 adversarial histories); the fault campaign covered
432 histories across secondary loss, primary election, and replication-path
partition. We specified schedule-specific predictions for each
configuration-property pair and compared them with recorded histories.

All normal controls passed. Under adversarial schedules, the configuration
using \texttt{local} reads, \texttt{w:1} writes, and no causal session produced
counterexamples for all four properties. With \texttt{majority} reads,
\texttt{majority} writes, and causal sessions, no adversarial history was
decidable: dependent reads timed out or write effects remained unresolved, so
these runs were not counted as PASS. Losing one secondary, or continuing after
a primary election, produced no violations in our schedules. Replication-path
partition produced 56 violations across four tested configuration-property
combinations. These results are limited to the tested schedules, timeout
settings, and single-host Docker deployment.
