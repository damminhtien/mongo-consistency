\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

This study examines four client-centric consistency properties on a three-member
MongoDB replica set: read-your-writes (RYW), monotonic reads (MR), monotonic
writes (MW), and writes-follow-reads (WFR). The configuration campaign varied
read concern, write concern, and causal sessions across eight settings. A
second campaign introduced a secondary crash, a primary election, and a
replication-path partition.

The normal controls contained \NormalPassCount\ PASS and
\NormalViolationCount\ VIOLATION outcomes in \NormalHistoryCount\ histories.
Among \AdversarialHistoryCount\ adversarial histories,
\AdversarialPassCount\ passed and \AdversarialViolationCount\ violated a
property. The remaining histories were unavailable, indeterminate, or missed
a precondition, so they were kept outside the resolved consistency comparison.
The fault campaign contained \RQTwoHistoryCount\ histories across
\RQTwoFaultEpisodeCount\ episodes. Under the partition, C1 returned stale or
reordered results while matching C6 histories usually ended at a majority-write
timeout before the dependent operation was issued. These observations apply to
MongoDB \MongoDBServerVersion, PyMongo \PyMongoVersion, the registered
schedules, and the single-host topology.
