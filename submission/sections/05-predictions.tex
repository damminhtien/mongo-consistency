\subsection{Fault expectations}

The first campaign changes settings while its schedule creates controlled
replica divergence. The second asks how the same client dependencies behave
when we change the topology.

\begin{description}
\item[F1, secondary loss] Removing one secondary should leave the primary and
a majority side intact. We expect the scheduled calls to complete without a
new consistency counterexample.
\item[F2, primary loss] Stopping the primary should trigger an election. We evaluate calls made after the election barrier; the schedule includes no call during the election. We expect the
surviving majority to restore service. However, a write acknowledged with \texttt{w:1} before the failure may be missing from subsequent operations if it had not reached the surviving members. 
\item[F3, replication partition] We isolate the current primary from both secondaries while preserving client access to it. Before detecting the partition and stepping down, the isolated member may still acknowledge \texttt{w:1} writes, but it cannot obtain majority acknowledgement for new writes. The other two
members can elect a primary whose state excludes those isolated-branch updates. Subsequent operations may therefore expose missing write or
read dependencies under configurations without the relevant guarantees.

\end{description}
