\section{Consistency Configurations}

\subsection{MongoDB consistency mechanisms}

RQ1 varies read concern, write concern, and causal-session setting.
Read concern governs which data state a read may return; write concern governs
the acknowledgement condition for a write. In a causally consistent session,
MongoDB documents combinations of these concerns with different client-centric
guarantees \cite[slides 49 to 53]{cai-lecture-3}
\cite{mongodb-read-concern,mongodb-write-concern,mongodb-read-preference,mongodb-causal-consistency,pymongo-sessions}.

Both causal-session conditions use an explicit PyMongo session. The OFF
condition sets causal consistency to false; it does not omit the session. The
read preference and requested member are selected by each registered schedule
to construct a stale or current observation. They are recorded per operation
but are not independent factors in C1-C8.

\subsection{Expected behaviour and predictions}

The prediction manifest assigns durability-aware targets to successful
histories under the registered schedules. C3 targets MR and WFR, C4 targets MW,
and C6 targets all four properties. C1, C2, C5, C7, and C8 have no registered
target. No target means that the project does not predict a counterexample on
every run. C5 separates majority concerns from causal-session setting; its
concerns alone are not treated as the project's registered causal guarantee.

These targets are committed in \texttt{configs/predictions.json} before the
campaign. A history classified UNAVAILABLE or INDETERMINATE does not establish
whether its consistency predicate held. The official MongoDB documentation
also distinguishes causal sessions and concern combinations, including the
durability condition on majority reads and writes
\cite{mongodb-causal-consistency}.

The four pre-registered hypotheses for RQ2, labelled H2.1 through H2.4, are:

\begin{description}
\item[H2.1] A secondary crash should reduce redundancy; operations that remain
routable should not create a new consistency anomaly.
\item[H2.2] A primary crash should appear as an election interval and possible
temporary unavailability rather than as a completed invalid history.
\item[H2.3] Isolating the former primary should expose differences between
weak and majority write acknowledgement, including rollback of an acknowledged
write on the isolated branch.
\item[H2.4] Majority concerns with causal sessions may turn a would-be
counterexample into a wait or an unresolved operation instead of a returned
stale value.
\end{description}

The comparison in Sections~5 and 6 keeps predictions separate from measured
outcomes. A pass in a finite campaign is not proof of a universal guarantee.
