\section{Discussion}

\subsection{From stale results to waiting and unresolved operations}

We interpret the contrast as a change in what the client can obtain, not as a
simple progression from failure to success. On a lagging branch, C1 can return
a value that contradicts the client's earlier operation. C6 often withholds
the answer or leaves a write unresolved. This is consistent with the roles of
majority acknowledgement and causal ordering, but the histories do not reveal
MongoDB's internal reason for every wait.

The paired histories explain how the controls changed the observations.
Read concern determined whether R1 could use the isolated branch's value;
write concern determined whether W1 could be acknowledged there; the causal
session carried a dependency into the next read. We can explain completed
counterexamples from the states used by the operations. A timeout, however,
does not expose the server's internal wait state.

\subsection{Consistency, completion, and latency}

The checker asks whether a completed history contradicted a dependency. We
also ask whether the client received a decidable answer and how long it waited
for the critical operation. During a partition, a setting that avoids a stale
answer may instead impose a timeout. Figure~\ref{fig:completion-latency}
places completion and latency beside the consistency result; it does not
represent a universal performance ranking.
