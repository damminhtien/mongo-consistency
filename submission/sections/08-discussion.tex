\section{Discussion}

\subsection{From stale results to waiting and unresolved operations}

All normal controls passed. Under the adversarial schedules, C1 produced a
completed violation for each property in all five trials. C6 produced no
completed counterexample: its RYW and MR histories were unavailable, and its
MW and WFR histories were indeterminate. A run without an observed violation
is therefore not always a PASS; the required operation may not complete or the
history may not provide a decidable result.

All \RQTwoViolationCount\ RQ2 violations occurred during F3, the
replication-path partition.
Under C6, the majority write often timed out before the dependent operation
could be issued. The timeout is a client observation; it does not establish
what internal state MongoDB was waiting for, and it is not a consistency pass.

The mechanism contrasts support this interpretation. In all eight valid M3
pairs, C3 acknowledged W1 at \texttt{w:1}, but W2's atomic pre-image lacked W1's
identifier; C6's majority W1 timed out in all eight pairs. In M2, C8's local
reads returned v1 in all eight pairs, and five dependent writes completed on a
v0 pre-image. The matched histories show the client-visible evidence for these
outcomes, but they do not expose MongoDB's internal wait state.

\subsection{Consistency, completion, and latency}

The property result answers whether a completed history contradicted the
dependency. The completion result asks whether the client obtained a
decidable answer at all. Latency then asks how long the critical operation
took. A stronger setting can therefore improve the consistency outcome while
reducing completion under partition.
