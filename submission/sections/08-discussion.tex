\section{Discussion}

\subsection{What the two campaigns show together}

The configuration and fault campaigns point to the same distinction. A weak
setting can return a completed value from a lagging branch or acknowledge a
write that is not retained after recovery. A stronger setting can withhold the
operation until the required state is available. The client then sees a wait,
timeout, or unresolved history instead of a returned stale value. These are
different outcomes, and the latter is not evidence of continuous availability.

The C1-C8 matrix is better read as an experimental cube than as a single
ranking. The result depends on the property predicate, operation order, session
state, read and write concern, and member that receives the request. Majority
read and majority write do not replace the causal dependency carried by a
session when the property concerns an ordered sequence of operations.

\subsection{Effect of topology changes}

The secondary crash removed redundancy without producing a recorded anomaly in
the tested schedule. Primary failure produced an election interval, but the
protocol did not send a subject operation during that interval. The partition
was the informative fault: it kept one branch client-reachable while removing
its replication path. C1 then exposed stale or reordered state, whereas C6
usually remained unresolved at the majority-write boundary.

\subsection{Completion and latency}

Completion and consistency must be reported separately. The completion tables
use PASS and VIOLATION as definitive outcomes and retain UNAVAILABLE and
INDETERMINATE as their own categories. All-attempt latency includes timed-out
critical operations; resolved latency uses only histories with a definitive
PASS or VIOLATION. This separation prevents a timeout from disappearing from
the measurement or being counted as a successful consistency check.

The matched mechanism histories support these explanations but do not reveal
MongoDB's internal wait state. A client timeout shows that the operation did
not return within the deadline. It does not by itself show whether the server
applied the operation, continued waiting, or later completed it.

\subsection{Interpretation boundary}

The observations are conditional on the recorded MongoDB and PyMongo versions,
the C1-C8 settings, the registered schedules, and the single-host Docker
topology. They describe the measured client histories. They do not establish a
universal guarantee for every deployment or application.
