\section{Discussion}

The discussion compares the committed prediction matrix with checked histories
for each configuration and property. It separates a successful counterexample
from an operation that was unavailable, a write whose effect is indeterminate,
a missed precondition, or a harness error. A precondition miss says that the
schedule did not construct the required state; it is not evidence for or
against a database guarantee.

Representative explanations use the saved operation trace: requested route,
actual server address, direct role observation, session timing, logical
dependency, fault event, and final independent snapshot. Driver topology cache
data are diagnostic only. Any explanation of a MongoDB mechanism must be
identified as an inference from these observations rather than a direct
inspection of internal state.

The normal controls are reported separately from the adversarial RQ1
comparison. The RC, WC, causal-session, and interaction contrasts are
exploratory summaries of available cells. They do not repair missing outcomes
or establish statistical significance.

A zero observed violation count means no counterexample appeared in the
completed histories for that registered schedule. It is not proof of a
universal guarantee. A high operation success rate is also distinct from
history-level consistency: availability and consistency are reported
separately.
