\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Predictions and observations}

The RQ1 prediction matrix and the RQ2 target/outcome table put each registered
expectation beside the checked histories. A prediction target applies to
successful histories under its schedule; it does not predict that every
repetition will pass. For targeted cells, completed counterexamples disagree
with the expected behavior. UNAVAILABLE and INDETERMINATE outcomes do not
resolve that comparison because the required value or dependent operation was
not observed. Non-target cells are descriptive, not failed predictions.

\subsection{Effect of consistency configuration}

The C1-C8 design varies read concern, write concern, and causal-session
setting. C3 targets MR and WFR, C4 targets MW, and C6 targets all four
properties. The observed cell matrix must be read with its durability
definition: a write acknowledged with \texttt{w:1} may later roll back, whereas
the project evaluates acknowledged writes as part of the recorded durable
history. C5 also shows why concern settings and causal-session state are
reported separately. The contrasts are descriptive and do not establish
statistical significance.

\subsection{Effect of node failure}

F1 removes a secondary but leaves the primary and one secondary. F2 removes the
primary and requires an election. All scheduled post-election F2 operations
completed. The election interval is reported separately, and election and
recovery quantiles use fault episodes rather than histories as their
denominator.

\subsection{Effect of network partition}

F3 preserves a client path to the isolated member while blocking its
replication path to the other two members. The C1 signature histories include
completed stale-read and write-order counterexamples, while the corresponding
C6 histories stop at majority-write timeouts and remain indeterminate. The
independent observer also records acknowledged writes that are absent after
recovery. These observations describe C1 and C6 under the tested topology.

\subsection{Consistency and availability}

Operation success and consistency outcome are separate measures. UNAVAILABLE
means an operation did not complete; INDETERMINATE means its effect or required
observation could not be resolved. Neither is a consistency pass. F2 has no
request during its election interval. F3 timeouts remain in all-attempt latency
quantiles rather than being dropped in favor of successful-call latency.

\subsection{RQ4 trade-off interpretation}

RQ4 treats consistency, observed definitive completion, and client-observed
time to the critical operation as separate measures. Under the tested
partition, C1's RYW P/V/U/I counts were \RQFourPartitionCOneRYWCounts, with
observed definitive completion \RQFourPartitionCOneRYWCompletion; its MW
signature was \RQFourPartitionCOneMWCounts, with
\RQFourPartitionCOneMWCompletion. The corresponding C6 RYW signature was
\RQFourPartitionCsixRYWCounts, with \RQFourPartitionCsixRYWCompletion, and
its MW signature was \RQFourPartitionCsixMWCounts, with
\RQFourPartitionCsixMWCompletion. These balanced rows support a conditional
statement for the recorded schedule; they do not rank configurations or
establish universal availability. RQ4 keeps all-attempt time, including valid
timeout outcomes, separate from resolved PASS/VIOLATION time. The C5/C6 normal
and RQ1 fault deltas in Table~\ref{tab:rq4-c5-c6} report the latency and
completion changes while holding the registered one-factor contrast fixed.
RQ3 supplies mechanism evidence for selected traces; RQ4 reports the recorded
client outcomes.

\subsection{Unexpected behavior and interpretation}

The C6 F3 majority-write timeouts match the registered expectation that stronger
durability may leave a dependent history waiting or unresolved rather than
return a stale value. A \texttt{w:1} write that is acknowledged and later
rolled back is recorded as a rollback under the project's durable-history
definition. The limits section gives the resulting boundary on interpretation.
