\section{Discussion}

The discussion compares each prediction with its checked main-campaign histories, then identifies the observable trace: member selection, returned version, election event, session ordering, waiting, rejection, or timeout. The requested member is kept separate from the actual server address, so a routing explanation can be checked against the command-monitor record. The factorial contrasts summarize read concern, write concern, causal-session, and interaction differences among adversarial histories with a decidable outcome.

\texttt{\AdversarialUnsupportedCount} of \texttt{\AdversarialHistoryCount} adversarial histories are \texttt{UNSUPPORTED} because the required topology precondition was not established: 54 MW and 46 WFR. The contrasts therefore describe the observed decidable histories, not the full planned matrix. No result from the pilot is pooled into these estimates.

All 15 adversarial RYW violations occurred in C1 (2/23 decidable histories), C7 (12/14), or C8 (1/30). C7 also had 16 RYW histories classified as \texttt{INDETERMINATE}. No violation was observed in the designated guarantee-target cells: C6 for RYW; C3 and C6 for MR and WFR; and C4 and C6 for MW. These finite outcomes do not prove those guarantees.

The two normal-control MR violations were \texttt{experiment-00664-C5-mr} and \texttt{experiment-00299-C8-mr}. In C5, the first read returned version 1 from \texttt{mongo3} and the second returned version 0 from \texttt{mongo1}; in C8, the reads returned versions 1 and 0 from \texttt{mongo2} and \texttt{mongo1}. Both histories used different secondary members and recorded no fault events. They are control observations, separate from the adversarial MR result.

The heatmap marks a cell \texttt{NA} when it has no \texttt{PASS} or \texttt{VIOLATION} history. MW and WFR in C7 have no such observation. Factorial contrasts omit those cells and are descriptive; they do not estimate a complete, balanced 2-by-2-by-2 matrix.

The factorial contrasts give equal weight to each available cell-level rate, despite different decidable-history counts across cells. They are descriptive contrasts, not inferential tests.

Latency quantiles pool histories collected in sequential and two-worker execution modes. They summarize this run on one host and should not be read as an isolated database-setting effect or as a comparison of independent machines.

A zero violation count means that no counterexample appeared under the tested workload and fault schedule. It does not support a claim about all MongoDB deployments. A high operation success rate also does not imply that returned histories satisfy every client-centric property. \texttt{UNAVAILABLE} and \texttt{INDETERMINATE} are availability or observation limits, not violations.
