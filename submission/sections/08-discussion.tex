\section{Discussion}

The discussion compares the committed prediction matrix with checked histories
for each configuration and property. It separates a successful counterexample
from an operation that was unavailable, a write whose effect is indeterminate,
a missed precondition, or a harness error. A precondition miss says that the
schedule did not construct the required state; it is not evidence for or
against a database guarantee.

Representative explanations use the saved operation trace: requested route,
actual server address, direct role observation, session timing, logical
dependency, fault event, and final independent snapshot. Driver topology cache
data are diagnostic only. Any explanation of a MongoDB mechanism must be
identified as an inference from these observations rather than a direct
inspection of internal state.

The RQ1 normal controls remain a separate baseline for both questions. RQ2
reports results by fault condition, configuration, and property; it does not
rerun or pool normal histories. Each grouped fault episode is one topology
event, and histories sharing that event are not counted as independent fault
events. The main RQ1 comparison follows configuration by property, from the
registered prediction to the checked observation and trace-supported
explanation. Descriptive factor contrasts are confined to the appendix and do
not establish statistical significance.

A zero observed violation count means no counterexample appeared in the
completed histories for that registered schedule. It is not proof of a
universal guarantee or a zero true violation probability. A high operation
success rate is distinct from history-level consistency. Scheduled-operation
success also does not measure continuous service availability.

For F2, the scheduled-operation success rate is not an estimate of service
availability across failover. Each history's first subject operation precedes
the primary crash; the next operation is issued only after the new primary is
elected. No request probes the election interval. All scheduled post-election
operations completed successfully, while the median election interval was
\RQTwoFtwoElectionMedianSeconds\ s across \RQTwoFtwoEpisodeCount\ episodes.
We therefore interpret the \RQTwoFtwoOperationSuccessPercent\% result as
success among scheduled calls, not as uninterrupted service during the election.
