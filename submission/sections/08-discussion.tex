\section{Discussion}

\subsection{From stale results to waiting and unresolved operations}

The configuration campaign shows that consistency settings change the form of
failure visible to the client. C7 provides the simplest normal example: a
\texttt{w:1} write returned successfully, but a later majority read without a
causal session reached a secondary that still returned version 0. The final
state converged to version 1, but convergence did not repair the value already
returned to the client.

The partition makes the contrast sharper. Under C1, a weak write can complete
on the isolated branch and the following operation can expose stale or
reordered state. Under C6, majority acknowledgement and the causal dependency
constrain the operation at the partition boundary. The client then sees a
timeout or an unissued dependent operation rather than a completed
counterexample. This is a change in failure mode, not evidence that the
operation succeeded.

The mechanism contrasts support this interpretation. C5 and C6 differ in
causal-session state; C8 and C5 differ in read concern; C3 and C6 differ in
write concern while preserving the other mechanism choices needed for the
comparison. The histories show what the client received and when, but they do
not expose MongoDB's internal wait state.

\subsection{Consistency, completion, and latency}

The property result answers whether a completed history contradicted the
dependency. The completion result asks whether the client obtained a
decidable answer at all. Latency then asks how long the critical operation
took. A stronger setting can therefore improve the consistency outcome while
reducing completion under partition.
