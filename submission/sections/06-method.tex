\subsection{Trial model and consistency checkers}

One trial uses a unique document, one explicit PyMongo session, and a
property-specific sequence of subject operations. The logical writer assigns
integer versions. Each write records its parent write, and each dependent
write records the version returned by its preceding read. The recorder keeps
the requested member, actual server, observed role, concern settings, session
state, operation status, operation time, and returned document state.

The subject client issues the tested operations. An independent observer reads
all three members after recovery without using the subject session. A
restricted controller applies the fault and replication-path changes. The
checker first verifies the attempted operations and their client-visible
evidence. RYW and MR use the versions returned to the subject client. MW uses
the preceding write identifier in the atomic pre-image returned to the
successive write, and WFR uses the application version in that pre-image. The
four checkers evaluate the course definitions in Section~2.1.

The post-heal observer is not used to infer MW or WFR. It records whether
acknowledged writes remain after recovery and whether replicas agree on the
final update list. Those durability observations are reported separately from
the client-centric consistency outcome.

\subsection{Configuration campaign}

To isolate the effect of consistency settings, the first campaign runs the
same property-specific schedules across all eight configurations. Three
repetitions per configuration and property form
\(3\times8\times4=96\) normal controls. Five repetitions form
\(5\times8\times4=160\) adversarial histories, for 256 histories in total.
Smoke and pilot histories are kept separate.

The schedules create the observation needed by each checker:

\begin{description}
\item[RYW] Keep a secondary at version 0, acknowledge a write of version 1
through the subject, and read from the stale secondary.
\item[MR] Isolate a secondary at version 0. If the other secondary is chained
through it, direct that member to replicate from the primary with
\texttt{replSetSyncFrom}~\cite{mongodb-replset-syncfrom} and verify the source
change. After a majority-acknowledged seed write, read version 1 from the fresh
secondary and then read from the stale secondary in the same client sequence.
\item[MW] Write on an isolated branch, allow the majority side to elect, and
issue a successor write after the topology change.
\item[WFR] Read a version on an isolated branch, wait for the majority-side
election, and issue a dependent write on the new primary.
\end{description}

In the fault campaign, MR creates a majority-acknowledged seed write outside
the subject session and requires the first subject read to return version~1
before the fault. For WFR, F1 and F2 also use a majority-acknowledged setup
write, independent of the subject configuration, before reading version~1.
F3 isolates the former primary,
creates the setup write with \texttt{w:1}, verifies version~1 on that branch
and version~0 on the majority side, and then reads version~1 before the
election. In every case, W2 is issued only after R1 has returned a concrete
version.

Each schedule checks its prerequisites before issuing the dependent subject
operation. If a prerequisite fails or returns no concrete version, the
dependent operation is not issued.

\subsection{Fault campaign}

The fault campaign crosses C1, C3, C4, and C6 with three topology conditions
and the four property schedules. F1 stops one secondary. F2 stops the primary,
waits for the remaining members to elect, and then resumes the scheduled calls.
F3 blocks replication traffic between the former primary and the other two
members while preserving the subject client's route to the isolated member.

The core design contains \(4\times3\times4\times8=384\) histories. Four F3
signature cells receive twelve additional repetitions each, giving
\RQTwoHistoryCount\ histories across \RQTwoFaultEpisodeCount\ shared fault
episodes. Histories in one episode share a topology event and are not
independent fault injections. Matching normal controls provide a descriptive
baseline; no extra normal campaign is added.

\subsection{Outcome classification and measurements}

Each history is classified as PASS, VIOLATION, UNAVAILABLE, INDETERMINATE,
PRECONDITION\_MISS, or HARNESS\_ERROR. Only PASS and VIOLATION are decidable
consistency outcomes. UNAVAILABLE records a failed client operation, while
INDETERMINATE records an unresolved effect or dependency. The other two
classes identify histories that did not reach the intended test or were not
valid harness executions.

For a set of histories, the resolved violation rate is
\[
V=\frac{\#\mathrm{VIOLATION}}
        {\#\mathrm{PASS}+\#\mathrm{VIOLATION}}.
\]
The decidable outcome rate is
\[
D=\frac{\#\mathrm{PASS}+\#\mathrm{VIOLATION}}
        {\#\mathrm{PASS}+\#\mathrm{VIOLATION}
        +\#\mathrm{UNAVAILABLE}+\#\mathrm{INDETERMINATE}}.
\]
The subject-operation success rate is
\[
S=\frac{\text{successful subject operations}}
        {\text{attempted subject operations}}.
\]

D describes completion of the registered workload. It is not formal CAP
availability. We report p50 and p95 operation latency, retain timed-out
operations in all-attempt samples when their times are available, and use
PASS/VIOLATION histories for resolved samples.
