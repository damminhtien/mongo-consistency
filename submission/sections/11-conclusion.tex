\section{Conclusion}

We set out to learn what a client sees when MongoDB's consistency controls
and replica-set topology change. Under normal operation, the tested
dependencies held. When we made replicas diverge, weak settings could
complete with stale or reordered state. Majority reads, majority writes, and
a causal session did not yield a completed counterexample in the target C6
histories, but those histories were often unresolved rather than successful.

Losing a secondary or probing after a primary election did not produce a
counterexample in our schedules. Partitioning the replication path did: it
let a weak client complete on a state that the majority side had not kept,
while stronger settings could wait or time out. The comparison supports our
main prediction about the change from a stale answer to a withheld one. It
does not establish behaviour during election or across physical sites beyond
this single-host experiment.
