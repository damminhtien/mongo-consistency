\section{Conclusion}

The configuration campaign answers the first study question: read concern,
write concern, and causal sessions change whether a stale or dependency-breaking
result can complete. The normal C7 history shows that a majority read does not
by itself protect a preceding \texttt{w:1} write when the session carries no
causal dependency.

The fault campaign answers the second question. The tested secondary loss and
post-election calls did not produce a new anomaly in their scheduled windows.
The replication partition created the clearest separation: C1 returned
completed stale or reordered outcomes, while C6 commonly stopped at a
majority-write timeout before the dependent operation could be issued.

The combined finding is that stronger settings can shift the client-visible
failure mode from an inconsistent completed result to waiting, timeout, or
unresolved completion. This conclusion is limited to the recorded schedules,
software versions, and single-host deployment.
