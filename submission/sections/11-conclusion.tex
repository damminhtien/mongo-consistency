\section{Conclusion}

We wanted to know what a client receives when we change MongoDB's consistency
settings and when a replica fails or replication is cut. In the normal runs,
all four checks passed. Once we made replicas disagree, weaker settings
sometimes returned stale reads or let a later write run without an earlier
one. C6 produced no completed counterexample in the tested schedules, but
its reads or writes often did not finish. We cannot count those unresolved
histories as passes.

Losing one secondary, or making calls after a new primary had been elected,
caused no consistency violation in our scheduled tests. Cutting replication
had a different effect: weaker settings completed with stale or reordered
state, while stronger settings made the client wait or time out. Avoiding a
stale answer can therefore mean not getting an answer yet. These findings
describe our schedules on one Docker host; they do not cover requests during
an election or failures across separate sites.
