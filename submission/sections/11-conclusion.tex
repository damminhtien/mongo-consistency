\section{Conclusion}

The configuration campaign answers the first study question. All normal
controls passed. Under the adversarial schedules, C1 produced violations for
all four properties in every trial. C6 produced unavailable RYW and MR
histories and indeterminate MW and WFR histories, rather than a completed
counterexample.

The fault campaign answers the second question. F1 and F2 produced no
violations among decidable histories; the F2 schedule resumed after the
election barrier and did not test calls during the election interval. F3
produced all \RQTwoViolationCount\ RQ2 violations and
\RQTwoIndeterminateCount\ indeterminate histories. Its C1 signature
cells returned stale or reordered values, while C6 majority-write timeouts
often prevented the dependent operation from being issued.

Across the tested schedules, stronger settings shifted some outcomes from
completed counterexamples to unavailable or indeterminate operations. These
results apply to the recorded schedules, software versions, and single-host
deployment.
