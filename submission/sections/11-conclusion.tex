\section{Conclusion}

We studied how MongoDB's consistency settings affect client operations during
normal operation and when replicas fail or lose contact. In the normal runs,
all four checks passed. Once replicas diverged, weaker settings
sometimes returned stale reads or allowed a later write to run without its
predecessor. With C6, we found no completed counterexample in the tested
schedules. Many reads and writes ended as UNAVAILABLE or INDETERMINATE, leaving
those histories without a completed consistency result.

When a secondary failed, or when we issued calls after a new primary had been
elected, none of the scheduled tests showed a consistency violation. During a
replication partition, however, the behaviour changed. Weaker settings
returned stale or reordered state, while stronger settings often waited or
timed out. As a result, some clients received an inconsistent value, while
others received no result before the timeout. The evidence covers the tested
schedules on one Docker host, with F2 requests issued after the election
barrier.
