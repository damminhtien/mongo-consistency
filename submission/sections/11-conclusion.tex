\section{Conclusion}

For Q1, changing MongoDB's consistency controls changed both what the client
could observe and whether dependent operations could complete. Some settings
exposed consistency violations, while others left the adversarial history
undecidable rather than producing a clear PASS.

For Q2, secondary loss and execution after a primary election produced no
violations in the tested schedules, while replication-path partition did. The
partition case was the scenario that exposed the clearest separation between
client-visible state and the state available on the majority side.

Overall, the experiments show that client-centric consistency cannot be judged
from observed violations alone. A history with no counterexample may still be
unavailable or undecidable, so operation completion has to be considered
alongside consistency.
