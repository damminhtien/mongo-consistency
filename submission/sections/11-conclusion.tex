\section{Conclusion}

The two campaigns show that concern settings and causal sessions change the
form of a consistency failure visible to a MongoDB client. Under the recorded
partition, C1 returned stale-read and write-order counterexamples. The matching
C6 histories usually stopped at a majority-write timeout before the dependent
operation could be issued. A stronger consistency setting can therefore avoid
a returned counterexample by leaving the operation unresolved.

The fault results also show why topology and completion need to be reported
alongside the property result. The election interval was not probed by a
subject request, and the post-recovery witness cannot describe every transient
state before convergence. The conclusions apply to MongoDB
\MongoDBServerVersion, PyMongo \PyMongoVersion, the recorded schedules, and
the single-host deployment. They are not a universal availability or
consistency guarantee.
