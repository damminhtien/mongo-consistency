\subsection{Read-your-writes consistency}

\subsubsection{RYW prediction}

The checker predicts that a read following a completed write by the same
client will not return an earlier version. C6 is the registered durable
causal-session target for RYW; the remaining cells have no RYW target.

\subsubsection{RYW experiment}

The RYW schedule writes version 1 through the subject session, then reads from
a directly verified stale secondary while a fresh replica has version 1. This
replica split creates a concrete test for a stale read without inferring state
from timestamps. The same property is exercised across the RQ2 fault matrix.

\subsubsection{RYW rationale}

The verified replica split means a completed read of version 0 directly
contradicts the preceding acknowledged version-1 write. This isolates the
property check from uncertainty about whether the stale state existed.

\subsubsection{RYW results}

RQ1 adversarial RYW histories comprise \RYWHistoryCount\ cases:
\RYWPassCount\ PASS, \RYWViolationCount\ VIOLATION,
\RYWUnavailableCount\ UNAVAILABLE, and
\RYWIndeterminateCount\ INDETERMINATE,
\RYWPreconditionMissCount\ PRECONDITION\_MISS, and
\RYWHarnessErrorCount\ HARNESS\_ERROR. The generated matrix reports every
configuration separately. RQ2 comprises \RQTwoRYWHistoryCount\ RYW histories:
\RQTwoRYWPassCount\ PASS, \RQTwoRYWViolationCount\ VIOLATION,
\RQTwoRYWUnavailableCount\ UNAVAILABLE, and
\RQTwoRYWIndeterminateCount\ INDETERMINATE,
\RQTwoRYWPreconditionMissCount\ PRECONDITION\_MISS, and
\RQTwoRYWHarnessErrorCount\ HARNESS\_ERROR.

In the F3 signature cells, C1 produced a stale-read violation in
\RQTwoFthreeConeRYWViolationCount/\RQTwoFthreeConeRYWHistoryCount\ histories.
C6 was INDETERMINATE in
\RQTwoFthreeCsixRYWIndeterminateCount/\RQTwoFthreeCsixRYWHistoryCount\
histories: its majority write timed out before the required read was issued.

\subsubsection{RYW explanation}

A completed read of version 0 after an acknowledged version-1 write is a
consistency counterexample. A timeout before the read produces no returned
value to compare and is therefore not a pass or violation. The C1/C6 partition
contrast in Table~\ref{tab:rq2-outcomes} reflects these distinct outcomes;
unavailability and consistency must not be conflated.

\subsection{Monotonic-reads consistency}

\subsubsection{MR prediction}

The prediction manifest targets MR in C3 and C6. Under the registered
durability-aware interpretation, successful later reads in those cells should
not regress below an earlier read in the same session.

\subsubsection{MR experiment}

The schedule first confirms that a fresh secondary contains version 1 while a
stale secondary remains at version 0. The client reads from the fresh member,
then the stale member, using the same explicit session. This order makes a
regression observable and gives the checker a concrete first-read version.

\subsubsection{MR rationale}

The first read is confirmed to return version 1 before the second route is
chosen. The checker can therefore distinguish a true regression from an
unobserved or missing setup write.

\subsubsection{MR results}

RQ1 adversarial MR histories comprise \MRHistoryCount\ cases:
\MRPassCount\ PASS, \MRViolationCount\ VIOLATION,
\MRUnavailableCount\ UNAVAILABLE, and
\MRIndeterminateCount\ INDETERMINATE,
\MRPreconditionMissCount\ PRECONDITION\_MISS, and
\MRHarnessErrorCount\ HARNESS\_ERROR. RQ2 comprises
\RQTwoMRHistoryCount\ MR histories: \RQTwoMRPassCount\ PASS,
\RQTwoMRViolationCount\ VIOLATION, \RQTwoMRUnavailableCount\ UNAVAILABLE,
\RQTwoMRIndeterminateCount\ INDETERMINATE,
\RQTwoMRPreconditionMissCount\ PRECONDITION\_MISS, and
\RQTwoMRHarnessErrorCount\ HARNESS\_ERROR.

The raw history \texttt{experiment-00003-C7-mr} is a completed RQ1
counterexample. Both reads used majority read concern in one session with
causal consistency disabled:

registered schedules, not proof of a general guarantee.

\subsection{Monotonic-writes consistency}

\subsubsection{MW prediction}

The prediction manifest targets MW in C4 and C6. A successful successor write
should not become visible after recovery without its predecessor under the
registered durable history definition.

\subsubsection{MW experiment}

The schedule sends the first subject write to an isolated old primary, waits
for the connected majority to elect a new primary, and sends a dependent
successor write there. The independent observer checks the complete document
on all three members after convergence. This tests write order across the
primary transition rather than relying on acknowledgements alone.

\subsubsection{MW rationale}

The first and successor writes execute on opposite sides of an election, and
the final converged update list reveals whether the later write survived
without its declared parent.

\subsubsection{MW results}

RQ1 adversarial MW histories comprise \MWHistoryCount\ cases:
\MWPassCount\ PASS, \MWViolationCount\ VIOLATION,
\MWUnavailableCount\ UNAVAILABLE, and
\MWIndeterminateCount\ INDETERMINATE,
\MWPreconditionMissCount\ PRECONDITION\_MISS, and
\MWHarnessErrorCount\ HARNESS\_ERROR. RQ2 comprises
\RQTwoMWHistoryCount\ MW histories: \RQTwoMWPassCount\ PASS,
\RQTwoMWViolationCount\ VIOLATION, \RQTwoMWUnavailableCount\ UNAVAILABLE,
\RQTwoMWIndeterminateCount\ INDETERMINATE,
\RQTwoMWPreconditionMissCount\ PRECONDITION\_MISS, and
\RQTwoMWHarnessErrorCount\ HARNESS\_ERROR.

In the F3 signature cells, C1 produced a write-order violation in
\RQTwoFthreeConeMWViolationCount/\RQTwoFthreeConeMWHistoryCount\ histories.
C6 was INDETERMINATE in
\RQTwoFthreeCsixMWIndeterminateCount/\RQTwoFthreeCsixMWHistoryCount\
histories because its first majority write timed out before the dependent
successor write was issued.

\subsubsection{MW explanation}

The C1 counterexample had a converged document containing successor \texttt{w2}
without predecessor \texttt{w1}. The C6 timeout leaves no completed two-write
history to order, so it is not a violation. The RQ1 table likewise keeps
completed write-order counterexamples separate from unresolved operations.

\subsection{Writes-follow-reads consistency}

\subsubsection{WFR prediction}

The prediction manifest targets WFR in C3 and C6. A write explicitly dependent
on a version returned by an earlier read should retain that version in the
converged state under the registered schedule.

\subsubsection{WFR experiment}

The schedule creates version 1 on the isolated old branch as setup, reads that
concrete version through the subject session, and issues a dependent write on
the newly elected primary. The checker compares the final document with the
recorded read dependency; it does not infer dependency from event ordering
alone.

\subsubsection{WFR rationale}

The explicit read identifier and version connect the dependent write to the
state actually returned to the client. The checker can test that dependency
after recovery without treating timestamp order as causal evidence.

\subsubsection{WFR results}

RQ1 adversarial WFR histories comprise \WFRHistoryCount\ cases:
\WFRPassCount\ PASS, \WFRViolationCount\ VIOLATION,
\WFRUnavailableCount\ UNAVAILABLE, and
\WFRIndeterminateCount\ INDETERMINATE,
\WFRPreconditionMissCount\ PRECONDITION\_MISS, and
\WFRHarnessErrorCount\ HARNESS\_ERROR. RQ2 comprises
\RQTwoWFRHistoryCount\ WFR histories: \RQTwoWFRPassCount\ PASS,
\RQTwoWFRViolationCount\ VIOLATION, \RQTwoWFRUnavailableCount\ UNAVAILABLE,
\RQTwoWFRIndeterminateCount\ INDETERMINATE,
\RQTwoWFRPreconditionMissCount\ PRECONDITION\_MISS, and
\RQTwoWFRHarnessErrorCount\ HARNESS\_ERROR. The per-configuration and
per-fault outcomes appear in Tables~\ref{tab:rq1-prediction-observation} and
\ref{tab:rq2-outcomes}.

\subsubsection{WFR explanation}

An acknowledged dependent write is evaluated against the exact version
returned to its subject session. A setup or read that fails its precondition
does not create a WFR test and is not converted into a violation. The outcome
matrix is the evidence for comparing targeted and non-targeted configurations.

\subsection{Cross-configuration and cross-scenario summary}

RQ1 completed \NormalHistoryCount\ normal controls and
\AdversarialHistoryCount\ adversarial histories. The RQ2 campaign completed
\RQTwoHistoryCount\ histories in \RQTwoFaultEpisodeCount\ fault episodes.
Its outcomes were \RQTwoPassCount\ PASS,
\RQTwoViolationCount\ VIOLATION,
\RQTwoUnavailableCount\ UNAVAILABLE, and
\RQTwoIndeterminateCount\ INDETERMINATE. The descriptive violation rate is
\RQTwoViolationCount/(\RQTwoPassCount+\RQTwoViolationCount) =
\RQTwoConsistencyViolationPercent\% among resolved histories. It describes
this fixed matrix, not a universal violation probability.

The RQ2 campaign recorded
\RQTwoOperationSuccessfulCount/\RQTwoOperationAttemptedCount\ successful
scheduled operations (\RQTwoOperationSuccessPercent\%) and a history
completion rate of \RQTwoHistoryCompletionPercent\%. Its all-attempt latency
p50/p95/p99 was \RQTwoLatencyMedianMs/\RQTwoLatencyHighMs/\RQTwoLatencyTailMs
ms. The independent observer checked
\RQTwoRollbackCheckedWriteCount\ acknowledged writes and found
\RQTwoRolledBackWriteCount\ rollbacks (\RQTwoRollbackPercent\%). Those
operation and rollback rates describe this campaign matrix, not real-world
probabilities.

F2 had \RQTwoFtwoOperationSuccessfulCount/\RQTwoFtwoOperationAttemptedCount\
successful scheduled operations and a median election interval of
\RQTwoFtwoElectionMedianSeconds\ s across \RQTwoFtwoEpisodeCount\ episodes.
The experiment issued no subject request during the election barrier, so this
success rate is not continuous availability. For F3, all-attempt p95 latency
was \RQTwoFthreeLatencyHighMs\ ms across
\RQTwoFthreeOperationAttemptedCount\ timed operations; this includes
\RQTwoFthreeIndeterminateCount\ timed-out operations. Among successful F3
operations, p95 was \RQTwoFthreeSuccessfulLatencyHighMs\ ms, with a different
denominator.
