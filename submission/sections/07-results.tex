\section{Results}

\subsection{Effect of consistency configuration}

We observed two outcomes under the adversarial schedules. Weak settings
sometimes completed with stale or reordered state. Stronger settings more often
produced UNAVAILABLE reads or INDETERMINATE writes.

All normal controls passed. Figure~\ref{fig:prediction-observation} compares
the adversarial observations with the configuration predictions. C1 returned
completed counterexamples for all four properties. C6 returned no decidable
history: the RYW and MR reads were unavailable, and the MW and WFR histories
were indeterminate. The complete matrix is in Appendix~\ref{app:full-results}.

The completed histories give uneven support for the predictions in
Table~\ref{tab:configurations}. C3-WFR produced completed passes. C3-MR,
C4-MW, and every C6 target lacked a decidable adversarial history, leaving
those predictions unevaluated. C4's completed WFR counterexample is a
descriptive observation; C4's prediction target is MW. For RYW and MR, the
visible contrast is especially clear: a weak setting can read from a lagging
member, whereas the stronger target may wait without returning an older value.
The MW and WFR checks use W2's atomic pre-image, not the post-recovery state.

\subsection{Effect of failures and network partition}

We next changed the topology to see whether the configuration pattern
persisted. The fault campaign contains \RQTwoHistoryCount\ histories across
\RQTwoFaultEpisodeCount\ shared fault episodes. Table~\ref{tab:fault-summary}
separates completed counterexamples from operations that did not supply a
decidable consistency result.

F1 and F2 produced no violation in the scheduled calls, as predicted for a
remaining majority and post-election requests. F2 included an election, but
we waited for its barrier before probing; the result says nothing about a
request made during that interval. F3 produced every fault-campaign violation
and indeterminate history. This matched the predicted difference between a
weak write accepted on an isolated branch and a majority write that timed out
there. C6 majority writes often timed out before the dependent operation.

The completed F3 counterexamples arose in C1 RYW and MW and in WFR under C1
and C4. The C4 WFR result needs care: we first wrote version 1 with
\texttt{w:1} on the isolated former primary, outside the subject session. C4's
local read returned it, but its dependent majority write reached the elected
primary, whose pre-image contained only version 0. The setup write used
\texttt{w:1} outside the subject session. C4's prediction target is MW, so its
WFR result is descriptive. The required WFR read returned no version 1 under
C3 and C6; those histories are classified PRECONDITION\_MISS.

\subsection{Mechanism evidence}

We replayed three paired contrasts to understand why the same fault could
give a value in one setting and no answer in another. With read and write
concerns held fixed, adding a causal session changed a stale C5 read into a
C6 read that carried \texttt{afterClusterTime} and timed out. Changing the read
concern from local (C8) to majority (C5) changed the version available to R1.
Changing the write concern from \texttt{w:1} (C3) to majority (C6) changed W1
from an acknowledged write later absent from W2's pre-image to a write that
timed out. The full paired outcomes are in Appendix~\ref{app:full-results}.
