\subsection{RQ2 fault campaign}

The RQ2 manifest state is \texttt{\RQTwoStatus}. The completed campaign contains
\RQTwoHistoryCount\ histories: \RQTwoPassCount\ PASS,
\RQTwoViolationCount\ VIOLATION, \RQTwoUnavailableCount\ UNAVAILABLE,
\RQTwoIndeterminateCount\ INDETERMINATE,
\RQTwoPreconditionMissCount\ PRECONDITION\_MISS, and
\RQTwoHarnessErrorCount\ HARNESS\_ERROR. The descriptive violation rate is
\RQTwoViolationCount/(\RQTwoPassCount+\RQTwoViolationCount) =
\RQTwoConsistencyViolationPercent\% among resolved histories. It describes this
fixed campaign matrix, not a universal violation probability. Subject
\RQTwoOperationSuccessfulCount/\RQTwoOperationAttemptedCount\ operations
succeeded (\RQTwoOperationSuccessPercent\%), and
\RQTwoHistoryCompletionPercent\% of histories reached a classified outcome.
Operation latency p50/p95/p99 was
\RQTwoLatencyMedianMs/\RQTwoLatencyHighMs/\RQTwoLatencyTailMs\ ms. The independent
observer checked \RQTwoRollbackCheckedWriteCount\ acknowledged writes and found
\RQTwoRolledBackWriteCount\ rollbacks (\RQTwoRollbackPercent\%). The rollback
rate describes writes in this fixed campaign and is not a real-world probability
estimate.

RQ2 used the matching C1/C3/C4/C6 RQ1 normal histories as a descriptive
baseline; it added no normal histories. The tables report every fault,
configuration, and property cell. Outcome vectors list, in order, PASS,
VIOLATION, UNAVAILABLE, INDETERMINATE, PRECONDITION\_MISS, and HARNESS\_ERROR.
Scheduled-operation success is reported as successful/attempted subject
operations; this ratio does not measure continuous service availability during
an interval with no request. Latency quantiles are in milliseconds. Rollback
counts use acknowledged writes as the observation unit.

All scheduled post-election operations completed successfully; the F2 failover
nevertheless introduced a median election interval of
\RQTwoFtwoElectionMedianSeconds\ s across \RQTwoFtwoEpisodeCount\ episodes,
during which the experiment deliberately issued no second subject operation.
The F2 operation-success rate therefore describes the calls that were
scheduled, not request success or uninterrupted service during election. The F2
operation-latency quantiles also cover issued calls only and exclude the election
barrier.

The latency table reports all subject operations with recorded start and end
times, regardless of outcome. For F3, p95 across all
\RQTwoFthreeOperationAttemptedCount\ timed operations was
\RQTwoFthreeLatencyHighMs\ ms; this includes the
\RQTwoFthreeIndeterminateCount\ timed-out INDETERMINATE
calls. Among the \RQTwoFthreeOperationSuccessfulCount\ successful F3 operations,
p95 latency was \RQTwoFthreeSuccessfulLatencyHighMs\ ms. These are different
denominators and answer different questions.

Four F3 signature cells distinguish the observed behavior. C1 RYW produced the
same stale-read violation in
\RQTwoFthreeConeRYWViolationCount/\RQTwoFthreeConeRYWHistoryCount\ histories;
C1 MW produced the same write-order violation in
\RQTwoFthreeConeMWViolationCount/\RQTwoFthreeConeMWHistoryCount\ histories. In
both cells an acknowledged version 1 was later read as version 0 (RYW), or
successor write \texttt{w2} was visible while predecessor \texttt{w1} was absent
(MW). C6 RYW and MW were INDETERMINATE in
\RQTwoFthreeCsixRYWIndeterminateCount/\RQTwoFthreeCsixRYWHistoryCount\ and
\RQTwoFthreeCsixMWIndeterminateCount/\RQTwoFthreeCsixMWHistoryCount\ histories,
respectively: the required majority write timed out before the dependent
operation was issued. These histories remain separate from consistency passes
and violations.

One C6 RYW history illustrates a protective outcome as unavailability rather
than a stale value. In \texttt{experiment-00018-C6-ryw}, W1 with majority write
concern succeeded on \texttt{mongo3:27017}. While \texttt{mongo1} remained a secondary with
replication isolated, the same causal session issued a majority read there.
The command carried the same \texttt{afterClusterTime} as W1 and timed out after five
seconds with \texttt{NetworkTimeout}; it returned no document. The checker labels the
history \texttt{UNAVAILABLE}, not a stale-read violation. This trace records the
client-visible result and does not expose MongoDB's internal causal counter.
