\section{Results}

\subsection{Effect of consistency configuration}

The configuration campaign separates two broad behaviours. Some weak settings
returned completed counterexamples under the adversarial schedules. Stronger
settings often stopped before returning an inconsistent value, leaving an
unavailable or indeterminate history instead.

The normal controls contained \NormalPassCount\ PASS and
\NormalViolationCount\ VIOLATION outcomes. The single VIOLATION is a genuine
normal-control observation, not a malformed record. In
\texttt{experiment-00192-C7-ryw}, C7 acknowledged version 1 on primary
\texttt{mongo2:27017} with \texttt{w:1}. The following majority read reached
secondary \texttt{mongo1:27017} without a causal session and returned version
0. The post-trial observer later found versions 0 and 1 on every member. The
checker therefore reports a completed stale read, exactly as the C7
descriptive setting permits.

The adversarial campaign contained \AdversarialPassCount\ PASS,
\AdversarialViolationCount\ VIOLATION, \AdversarialUnavailableCount\
UNAVAILABLE, \AdversarialIndeterminateCount\ INDETERMINATE,
\AdversarialPreconditionMissCount\ PRECONDITION\_MISS, and
\AdversarialHarnessErrorCount\ HARNESS\_ERROR outcomes. The precondition misses
occurred in MR histories and are excluded from the decidable comparison.

The RYW and MR schedules show the clearest separation between a completed
regression and an unresolved dependency. Weak or non-causal settings can reach
a lagging member and return an older value; the stronger target setting more
often stops before a stale value is returned. MW is decided from whether the
preceding write identifier is present in the successive write's atomic pre-image.
WFR is decided from the application version present in that pre-image. Histories
without that evidence remain INDETERMINATE; the post-heal witness is used only
for the separate durability analysis.

\subsection{Effect of failures and network partition}

The fault campaign tests whether the configuration effects persist when the
topology changes. It contains \RQTwoHistoryCount\ histories across
\RQTwoFaultEpisodeCount\ fault episodes, with
\RQTwoPassCount\ PASS, \RQTwoViolationCount\ VIOLATION,
\RQTwoUnavailableCount\ UNAVAILABLE, and \RQTwoIndeterminateCount\
INDETERMINATE outcomes.

F1, the secondary loss, removed redundancy but did not produce a new
consistency anomaly in the scheduled calls. F2, the primary loss, produced an
election and recovery; the protocol resumed calls after the election barrier,
so it does not measure a request issued during that interval. F3, the
replication-path partition, produced the clearest separation. C1 completed
stale or reordered outcomes in the signature cells, while C6 commonly timed
out at the majority-write boundary before the dependent operation was issued.

\subsection{Mechanism evidence}

The campaign-level patterns can be explained by three controlled contrasts.
The paired histories change causal-session state, read concern, or write
concern while keeping the mechanism comparison explicit. They connect the
returned value or timeout to the route and session metadata recorded in the
history.
